# Projects & Scholarly Work

My professional and scholarly work brings together curriculum design, literary studies, rhetoric and composition, and emerging interests in Digital Humanities. Through secondary and postsecondary teaching, I have developed instructional materials, contributed to curriculum planning, presented professional development sessions, and pursued research examining literature, adaptation, and cultural representations of transformation.

This page highlights some of my selected professional projects, scholarly presentations, and developing areas of research.

## Curriculum Design & Instructional Development

My instructional design work focuses on creating purposeful learning experiences that support critical thinking, student engagement, rhetorical awareness, and analytical writing. Across secondary and postsecondary settings, I develop instructional materials and scaffolded assignments that guide students through increasingly complex reading, writing, and research tasks.

- <strong>Composition and Rhetoric:</strong> Rhetorical analysis, research literacy, source evaluation, revision, and writing for different audiences and purposes.
- <strong>Literary Studies:</strong> Thematic units that connect close reading and literary analysis with historical, cultural, and philosophical contexts.
- <strong>Scaffolded Learning:</strong> Sequenced assignments and checkpoints that help students develop ideas, evaluate sources, and build toward larger projects.
- <strong>Digital and Multimodal Learning:</strong> Instructional activities that engage students with digital environments, visual texts, and multiple forms of communication.

## Literary Research & Adaptation Studies

My graduate research explored adaptation and the representation of human and nonhuman transformation. My master's research project, *“Performing Adaptation: The Aesthetics of (Non)Human Transformation in the Annihilations”* (2025), examined questions of adaptation and transformation through literary and screen-based texts.

This work informs my continuing interests in adaptation studies, environmental humanities, speculative fiction, and representations of ecological change.

## Digital Humanities & Emerging Research

As a PhD student in Texts and Technology at the University of Central Florida, I am developing research interests at the intersection of literature, technology, digital culture, and rhetoric and composition.

- <strong>Digital Narratives:</strong> Nonlinear, fragmented, and participatory storytelling across digital platforms.
- <strong>Digital Literacy & Rhetoric:</strong> How people compose, interpret, and circulate meaning across digital environments.
- <strong>Literature, Adaptation & the Anthropocene:</strong> How literary and screen-based narratives represent ecological crisis, transformation, and uncertain futures.
- <strong>Digital Humanities Methods:</strong> How digital tools and research methods can support new approaches to humanities questions.
