# Teaching

My teaching practice brings together composition, literature, rhetorical analysis, and multimodal literacy. Across secondary and college classrooms, I focus on helping students become more confident readers, writers, researchers, and critical thinkers. I am particularly interested in how scaffolded instruction, thoughtful course design, and meaningful engagement with texts can make learning more accessible while encouraging students to take intellectual risks.

## Instructional Design & Curriculum Development

My approach to course design emphasizes clear expectations, scaffolded learning, and alignment between course objectives, instruction, and assessment. I develop assignments that break complex tasks into manageable stages, giving students opportunities to practice skills, apply feedback, and build toward more independent work.

My instructional interests include:

- <strong>Composition and Rhetorical Analysis:</strong> Helping students understand how purpose, audience, genre, and context shape writing and communication.
- <strong>Scaffolded Writing Instruction:</strong> Designing assignments that move students from brainstorming and planning to drafting, research, revision, and reflection.
- <strong>Multimodal Literacy:</strong> Exploring how meaning is constructed through written, visual, digital, and other forms of communication.
- <strong>Research and Information Literacy:</strong> Supporting students as they develop research questions, evaluate sources, synthesize information, and communicate evidence-based arguments.
- <strong>Literature and Critical Reading:</strong> Encouraging close reading, interpretation, and engagement with the cultural and historical contexts of literary texts.
- <strong>Online Course Design:</strong> Developing structured learning experiences, instructional materials, and interactive assignments for online and accelerated courses.

## My Teaching Philosophy

My teaching philosophy is grounded in the belief that writing should be something students learn to do, not something they are expected to already know how to do. I am interested in debarriering writin -- making the expectations, conventions, and processes of writing more accessible to students while helping them develop confidence in their own voices. Too often, academic writing can feel like a set of unwritten rules that students are expected to navigate on their own. My goal is to make those rules visible, give students the tools to work with them, and help them understand that writing is a process of discovery, practice, and revision.

My experience teaching in a Title I school district, along with my work as an English Language Arts Department Chair, has shaped the way I approach instruction, life, and everything. Working with students from a range of backgrounds has reinforced the importance of meeting students where they are while still challenging them to grow. Students do not all enter the classroom with the same experiences, resources, or familiarity with academic expectations. I see it as my responsibility to create meaningful opportunities for students to build those skills rather than assume they already have them. I can’t teach *Prometheus Unbound* if my students don’t even know Prometheus is the guy who stole fire.

In both secondary and college classrooms, I emphasize scaffolded instruction, critical reading, rhetorical awareness, and authentic engagement with texts. I break larger assignments into manageable steps, model the choices writers make, and encourage students to see revision as an essential part of learning. I also want students to recognize that writing extends beyond the traditional academic essay. Digital texts, visual media, public writing, and everyday communication all offer opportunities to think critically about how meaning is created and how audiences are engaged. Film and video games are pretty fly and offer opportunity for reimagination of critical thinking processes
